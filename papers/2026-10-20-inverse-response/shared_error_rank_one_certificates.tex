\begin{abstract}
We study upper certificates for $\ip f{H^{-1}f}$ when $H$ is a
self-adjoint operator bounded below by a positive constant and the action
on a trial vector is known with error. Retaining the same error vector in
the energy and residual terms gives a joint norm-ball bound, which admits
a padded Gram implementation and exact strict comparisons without
numerical square roots. A rank-one lower bound for the inverse slack gives
two equivalent exact-action corrections and an exact complex rescaling
formula; uniform interval relaxations of those corrections need not
coincide. We prove a precision-only capacity bound and finite-rank
Schur-complement and single-target reductions, without evaluating them.
Two previously completed tests at shift $1/128$ are documented. The first
excludes its fixed shared-error majorant, and its stronger common-base
inequality also excludes the specified fixed-coefficient Young families.
The second certifies positive rank-one corrections but remains
inconclusive for the target inequality. All statements about the theta
application retain the physical operator, domain, Gram, and action
inclusions as conditional premises. No new scientific computation or
conclusion about the Riemann hypothesis is supplied.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting and scope}\label{sec:setting}

Let $\HH$ be a nonzero complex Hilbert space, with inner product conjugate-linear
in the first argument. Throughout,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coercive}
 H=H^*,\qquad \Dom(H)\ \text{dense in }\HH,\qquad H\succeq aI,\quad a>0.
\end{equation}
Thus $H^{-1}$ is a bounded positive operator on $\HH$. For $f\in\HH$ the
quantity to be bounded is
\[
 \Iresp=\ip f{H^{-1}f}.
\]
The general arguments require only \eqref{eq:coercive} and the stated
finite action information. They do not require a trial vector in
$\Dom(H^2)$.

For the saved finite model, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maps}
 V:\C^{68}\longrightarrow\Dom(H),\qquad
 O:\C^{372}\longrightarrow\HH,\qquad
 G,E:\C^{68}\longrightarrow\HH,
 \qquad HV=G-hV+E,
\end{equation}
where $h=1/128$. The positive coercivity constant $a$ is distinct from
$h$. Fix $C\in\C^{68\times372}$ and a nonzero $v\in\C^{372}$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vectors}
\begin{gathered}
 c=Cv,\quad n=v^*v,\quad t_c=c^*c,\quad f=Ov,\quad w=Vc,\\
 G_h=G-hV,\quad g=G_hc,\quad r=f-g,\quad z=Ec.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In particular $Hw=g+z$ and the true residual is $r-z$.
The saved direction has 211 stored coordinates followed by 161 exact
zeros. The coefficient matrix and that direction remain fixed in both
reported tests. The target is exactly $\Iresp<n$; there is no additional
$1-\delta$ factor.

The admitted norm and error budgets are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budgets}
 \norm V\le\nu,\qquad E^*E\preceq\sigma^2 I,\qquad
 \norm E\le\sigmab,\qquad
 B_i\ge\norm{E_i},\quad
 \sum_{i=1}^{68}B_i^2=\sigma^2\le\sigmab^2.
\end{equation}
Here $E_i$ is the $i$th error column and $\sigma\ge0$ denotes the
square root of the stored nonnegative number $\sigma^2$.
The order is the original 64 columns followed by four appended columns.
The triangle and Cauchy--Schwarz inequalities give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:e}
 \norm z\le e:=\sum_{i=1}^{68}B_i|c_i|
 \le \sigma\sqrt{t_c}\le\sigmab\sqrt{t_c}.
\end{equation}
Thus $e$ is the smallest of these three admitted bounds. This conclusion
uses the ordered sum-of-squares identity in \eqref{eq:budgets}; it is not
a general comparison between unrelated error estimates. The saved
real rational model makes $e$ exact rational. This paper reuses its
stored value and does not repeat the sum.

\subsection{Conditional theta interpretation}

To identify the inherited realization, define for $x\ge0$
\[
 \kappa_\theta(x)=e^{x/2}\sum_{j\ge1}
 (4z_j(x)^2-6z_j(x))e^{-z_j(x)},\qquad
 z_j(x)=\pi j^2e^{2x},
\]
and extend evenly. With $c_\theta(x)=\cosh(x/2)$, the inherited Hilbert
space is $L^2_{\rm even}(\R,\nu_\theta;\C)$, where
$\dd\nu_\theta=2\kappa_\theta c_\theta\,\dd x$ and
$\nu_\theta(\R)=1$. The symmetric half measure is
\[
 \dd\mu(s)=\frac12\frac{e^{-|s|/2}}{1-e^{-2|s|}}\dd s+
 \frac12\sum_{j\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(j)}{\sqrt j}
       (\delta_{\log j}+\delta_{-\log j}).
\]
Here $\Lambda(j)$ is $\log p$ when $j$ is a positive power of a prime
$p$, and zero otherwise.
Its continuous part, whose density is singular at $s=0$, is paired with
a difference:
\[
 \mathcal E_\theta(u)=
 \int_{\R}\int_{\R}\kappa_\theta(x)\kappa_\theta(x-s)
       |u(x)-u(x-s)|^2\,\dd x\,\dd\mu(s).
\]
Let $A_\theta$ represent the minimum closure of the compact smooth even
core in the $\mathcal E_\theta+\HH$ norm. The inherited theta operator is
\[
 \mathcal L=A_\theta-\tfrac12 I+\tfrac12 P_1,\qquad
 P_1u=\ip1u\,1,\qquad \Dom(\mathcal L)=\Dom(A_\theta).
\]
The action anchor and shifted operator are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thetaH}
 H_0=\mathcal L+\tfrac1{64}I+OO^*,\qquad
 H=H_0-hI=\mathcal L+\tfrac1{128}I+OO^*.
\end{equation}
$G$ approximates $H_0V$, which explains the $-hV$ in
\eqref{eq:maps}. This is a minimum closed realization; no identification
with a maximal domain is used.

\begin{hypothesis}[Inherited physical premises]\label{hyp:physical}
For the theta application, the infinite source positivity,
normalization, analytic and convergence properties needed for the
displayed construction are assumed. The minimum form and operator,
the source identities, and the cover and omitted-prime estimates giving
\eqref{eq:coercive} are also assumed. All 68 true trial functions belong
to $\Dom(H)$. The full-line action approximants and their error ownership,
including exterior contributions, satisfy \eqref{eq:maps}--\eqref{eq:budgets}.
Finally, the retained raw Gram inclusions enclose the actual physical
inner products, including integration, truncation, source, transfer,
and serialization errors.
\end{hypothesis}

Neither file identity, a historical status word, nor agreement in a
review proves Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:physical}. The present work does not
recertify it. The abstract theorems below are unconditional under their
explicit Hilbert-space assumptions; their use for the saved theta
instance is conditional on these physical premises and correct input
bindings.

\section{A joint bound for a shared action error}\label{sec:joint}

\begin{theorem}[Residual identity and joint ball bound]\label{thm:joint}
With \eqref{eq:coercive} and \eqref{eq:vectors}, define
\[
 d_0=2\Re\ip fw-\Re\ip wg,\qquad s=w+\frac2a r.
\]
If $\norm z\le e$, then
\begin{align}
 \Iresp
 &\le d_0+\frac{\norm r^2}{a}+\frac{\norm z^2}{a}
                  -\Re\ip sz \notag\\
 &\le Q_{\rm joint}:=d_0+\frac{\norm r^2}{a}
                         +\frac{e^2}{a}+e\norm s.       \label{eq:joint}
\end{align}
More precisely, with $T=a^{-1}I-H^{-1}\succeq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jointgap}
 Q_{\rm joint}-\Iresp
 =\frac{e^2-\norm z^2}{a}
  +e\norm s+\Re\ip sz+\ip{r-z}{T(r-z)}.
\end{equation}
The maximum of $\norm{\zeta}^2/a-\Re\ip s\zeta$ over the relaxed ball
$\norm\zeta\le e$ is $e^2/a+e\norm s$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any $w\in\Dom(H)$, expansion of the bounded inverse gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residentity}
 \Iresp=2\Re\ip fw-\ip w{Hw}
             +\ip{f-Hw}{H^{-1}(f-Hw)}.
\end{equation}
Replace $H^{-1}$ by $a^{-1}I-T$, use $Hw=g+z$, and expand
$\norm{r-z}^2$. This gives the first inequality and the exact identity
\eqref{eq:jointgap}. Each term on its right is nonnegative after
grouping $e\norm s+\Re\ip sz\ge0$. The stated maximum is attained at
$\zeta=-es/\norm s$ if $s\ne0$. If $s=0$ and the Hilbert space is
nonzero, any vector of norm $e$ attains it; the zero-radius case is
immediate.
\end{proof}

The maximizing relaxed error need not be compatible with a single
self-adjoint $H$, with its other prescribed actions, or with the physical
column errors. Ball sharpness is a statement about that relaxation.

\begin{proposition}[Gain over separated error terms]\label{prop:gain}
Replacing $e\norm{w+2r/a}$ by
$e\norm w+2e\norm r/a$ loses exactly
\[
 G_{\rm geom}=e\left(\norm w+\frac2a\norm r
                             -\norm{w+2r/a}\right)\ge0.
\]
It is strictly positive if and only if
$e>0$ and $\Re\ip wr<\norm w\,\norm r$. Moreover,
\[
 0\le G_{\rm geom}\le
 2e\min\left(\norm w,\frac2a\norm r\right).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The triangle inequality proves nonnegativity. Equality in it is equivalent
to $\Re\ip wr=\norm w\,\norm r$, as follows by squaring both nonnegative
sides; this includes either vector being zero. The reverse triangle
inequality bounds the deficit by
$\norm w+2\norm r/a-|\norm w-2\norm r/a|$, giving the upper bound.
\end{proof}
This exact geometric improvement does not imply a strict visible
improvement between independently widened numerical endpoints.

\section{Padded Grams and exact strict tests}\label{sec:cap}

Define the physical Gram matrices
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grams}
 M=O^*O,\quad D=V^*O,\quad K=G^*O,\quad
 N_g=G^*G,\quad F_g=\Herm(V^*G),\quad P=V^*V,
\end{equation}
where $\Herm X=(X+X^*)/2$. Let $\Pup$ be a fixed exact matrix with
$P\preceq\Pup$. The five real primitive contractions and the padded
trial value are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalars}
\begin{gathered}
 m=v^*Mv,\quad x=\Re(c^*Dv),\quad u=\Re(c^*Kv),\\
 b=c^*N_gc,\quad y=c^*F_gc,\quad
 P_0=c^*\Pup c,\quad p_0=\norm w^2,\quad
 \ell_P=P_0-p_0\ge0.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The scalar $b$ is not the vector $b_H$ used later.
$P_0$ already includes the admitted Gram padding once; it is neither
$p_0$ nor the unknown slack $\ell_P$.

\begin{theorem}[Padded, capped shared-error bound]\label{thm:cap}
Set
\begin{align}
 R&=m-2u+2hx+b-2hy+h^2P_0, \notag\\
 D_0&=2x-y+hP_0,\qquad L=D_0+R/a, \notag\\
 J&=P_0+\frac4a(x-y+hP_0)+\frac4{a^2}R,       \label{eq:RLJ}\\
 W^2&=\min(\nu^2t_c,P_0),\qquad
 j=\min\left(\sqrt J,\sqrt{W^2}+\frac2a\sqrt R\right). \notag
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paddedidentity}
 R=\norm r^2+h^2\ell_P,\quad
 D_0=d_0+h\ell_P,\quad
 J=\norm s^2+(1+2h/a)^2\ell_P,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap}
 \Iresp\le\Qcap:=L+\frac{e^2}{a}+ej
 \le Q_{\rm old}^*
 :=D_0+\nu\sigmab t_c+
                  \frac{(\sqrt R+\sigma\sqrt{t_c})^2}{a}.
\end{equation}
$Q_{\rm old}^*$ is the infimum over $\tau>0$ of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau}
 Q_\tau=D_0+\nu\sigmab t_c+
       \frac{(1+\tau)R+(1+\tau^{-1})\sigma^2t_c}{a}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Expand $\norm{f-Gc+hw}^2$ and $\norm{w+2r/a}^2$ to obtain
\eqref{eq:paddedidentity}. Both $\sqrt{W^2}$ and $\nu\sqrt{t_c}$
bound $\norm w$; $\sqrt R$ bounds $\norm r$. Consequently both
arguments of the minimum defining $j$ bound $\norm s$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:joint} therefore gives the first inequality in
\eqref{eq:cap}. For the second, use
$j\le\nu\sqrt{t_c}+2\sqrt R/a$ together with
$e^2\le\sigma^2t_c$, $e\le\sigma\sqrt{t_c}$, and
$e\le\sigmab\sqrt{t_c}$ in the three corresponding terms.
Finally
$\inf_{\tau>0}(\tau R+\tau^{-1}\sigma^2t_c)
 =2\sqrt{R\sigma^2t_c}$, by completing a scalar square.
The infimum statement also holds when one factor is zero, whether or
not it is attained at a finite $\tau$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Collecting coefficients before taking radii}

Suppose each $q_i$ in $(m,x,u,b,y)$ lies in a real ball
$[\widehat q_i-\epsilon_i,\widehat q_i+\epsilon_i]$.
For a linear form $\ell=\ell_0+\sum_i\alpha_iq_i$, use center
$\ell_0+\sum_i\alpha_i\widehat q_i$ and radius
$\sum_i|\alpha_i|\epsilon_i$ \emph{after} collecting repeated
coefficients. The required rows are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc|c}
 &m&x&u&b&y&\text{constant}\\ \hline
 R&1&2h&-2&1&-2h&h^2P_0\\
 D_0&0&2&0&0&-1&hP_0\\
 L&1/a&2(1+h/a)&-2/a&1/a&-(1+2h/a)&h(1+h/a)P_0\\
 J&4/a^2&(4/a)(1+2h/a)&-8/a^2&4/a^2&
             -(4/a)(1+2h/a)&(1+2h/a)^2P_0
\end{array}
\]
This preserves cancellation between repeated occurrences of a primitive.
It does not assert independence of the primitive errors.

Write $L_\pm,R_\pm,J_\pm$ for the resulting endpoints. Because the
physical $R,J$ are nonnegative, a valid raw lower endpoint may be replaced
by its maximum with zero. A negative upper endpoint, reversed interval,
negative $P_0,t_c,e,W^2$, or nonpositive $a,n$ is a consistency
\hold, not a value to clip or an ordinary failed target test.
The saved implementation uses the collected $L$ enclosure without an
additional intersection with an independently widened $D_0+R/a$.

\subsection{Strict rational predicates}

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symbolic}
\begin{gathered}
 A_\pm=L_\pm+e^2/a,\qquad X=e^2W^2,\qquad
 Y_\pm=4e^2R_\pm/a^2,\qquad Z_\pm=e^2J_\pm,\\
 Q_\pm=\min(A_\pm+\sqrt{Z_\pm},
                   A_\pm+\sqrt X+\sqrt{Y_\pm}).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Monotonicity gives $Q_-\le\Qcap\le Q_+$.

\begin{lemma}[Sign before squaring]\label{lem:strict}
For $X,Y,Z\ge0$ and real $A,n$, put $d=n-A$ and
$k=d^2-X-Y$. The exact strict comparisons are
\begin{align*}
 A+\sqrt Z<n
 &\iff d>0\ \text{and}\ Z<d^2,\\
 A+\sqrt Z>n
 &\iff d<0\ \text{or}\ (d\ge0\ \text{and}\ Z>d^2),\\
 A+\sqrt X+\sqrt Y<n
 &\iff d>0,\ k>0,\ k^2>4XY,\\
 A+\sqrt X+\sqrt Y>n
 &\iff d<0\ \text{or}\
   \bigl(d\ge0\ \text{and}\
       [\,k<0\ \text{or}\ (k\ge0\ \text{and}\ 4XY>k^2)\,]\bigr).
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a single root, isolate the nonnegative root and square only when the
threshold has the required sign. For two roots and $d\ge0$, the comparison
after the first squaring is between $2\sqrt{XY}$ and
$k=d^2-X-Y$. Its sign is checked before the second squaring. For the
strict lower comparison $d$ and then $k$ must be positive. These steps
also prove the displayed alternatives when a radicand or threshold is
zero.
\end{proof}

One upper branch strictly below $n$ suffices for a conditional directional
\pass. Both lower branches strictly above $n$ exclude this fixed
$\Qcap$ as a certificate for this direction. Exact equality is
inconclusive. Exclusion of an upper majorant is not a lower bound on
$\Iresp$.

\section{What a common base excludes}\label{sec:families}

There is a stronger conclusion when the base alone exceeds the target.
It must be proved from that base inequality, not from failure of an
upper test. We now specify the families to which it applies.

For fixed $C$, define the Hermitian matrices
\begin{align}
 R_{\rm up}
 &=M-2\Herm(C^*K)+2h\Herm(C^*D)+C^*N_gC
                    -2hC^*F_gC+h^2C^*\Pup C,\notag\\
 D_{\rm up}
 &=2\Herm(C^*D)-C^*F_gC+hC^*\Pup C,\notag\\
 J_{\theta,{\rm up}}
 &=\theta^2C^*\Pup C+
   \frac{4\theta}{a}
       \bigl[\Herm(C^*D)-C^*F_gC+hC^*\Pup C\bigr]
       +\frac4{a^2}R_{\rm up}.                  \label{eq:matrixpads}
\end{align}
Here $0\le\theta\le1$. If
$S_\theta=\theta VC+2(O-G_hC)/a$, then
$J_{\theta,{\rm up}}\succeq S_\theta^*S_\theta$.
Indeed their difference is
$(\theta+2h/a)^2C^*(\Pup-P)C$.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-coefficient Young families]\label{prop:families}
For $\lambda>0$ and $0\le\theta\le1$, the matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ufamily}
\begin{split}
 U_{\theta,\lambda}
 ={}&D_{\rm up}+R_{\rm up}/a\\
 &+\left[\sigma^2/a+(1-\theta)\nu\sigmab+
                      \lambda\sigma^2/2\right]C^*C
       +J_{\theta,{\rm up}}/(2\lambda)
\end{split}
\end{equation}
satisfies $O^*H^{-1}O\preceq U_{\theta,\lambda}$.
For the fixed $v$ of \eqref{eq:vectors},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:basefamily}
 v^*U_{\theta,\lambda}v
 \ge L+\sigma^2t_c/a
 \ge L+e^2/a.
\end{equation}
Consequently $L_-+e^2/a>n$ excludes every member of this family at
this same $C,v$. The choice $\theta=0$, $\lambda=2/(a\tau)$ gives
\eqref{eq:tau}; $\theta=1$ gives the fully joint Young family.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the first line of \eqref{eq:joint}, split
$s=\theta w+2r/a+(1-\theta)w$. Bound the remaining linear term by
$(1-\theta)\nu\sigmab t_c$, and apply
\[
 -\Re\ip{\theta w+2r/a}z
 \le\frac{\norm{\theta w+2r/a}^2}{2\lambda}
       +\frac{\lambda}{2}\norm z^2.
\]
Use $\norm z^2\le\sigma^2t_c$ and then the padded identities.
The total coefficient of the trial-Gram padding is
\[
 h+\frac{h^2}{a}+\frac{(\theta+2h/a)^2}{2\lambda}>0,
\]
so this substitution preserves the upper inequality.
The argument applies to every observation coefficient vector, proving
the matrix bound. For the fixed $v$, the extra terms beyond
$L+\sigma^2t_c/a$ are nonnegative, since
$J_{\theta,{\rm up}}\succeq0$. Equation~\eqref{eq:e} proves the second
inequality in \eqref{eq:basefamily}. Substitution of
$\theta=0,\lambda=2/(a\tau)$ gives \eqref{eq:tau}.
\end{proof}

This conclusion does not range over other coefficients, other trial or
observation maps, different error models, or inverse-slack corrections.
It supplies no negative direction of the true theta operator.

\section{One exact action and inverse slack}\label{sec:rankone}

For this section take any $w\in\Dom(H)$ with its \emph{exact} action,
and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rankdefs}
 b_H=(H-aI)w,\quad
 \kappa=\ip w{b_H}\ge0,\quad
 B=a\kappa+\norm{b_H}^2,\quad D_H=aB.
\end{equation}
The letter $B$ in this section is a scalar, distinct from the column
bounds $B_i$. Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F}
 F(w)=2\Re\ip fw-\ip w{Hw}+\frac{\norm{f-Hw}^2}{a}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Rank-one inverse-slack certificate]\label{thm:rankone}
If $B>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverseorder}
 H^{-1}\preceq a^{-1}I-\frac{b_Hb_H^*}{D_H}.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{align}
 \Iresp&\le \frac{\norm f^2}{a}
                    -\frac{|\ip{b_H}f|^2}{D_H},\label{eq:directexact}\\
 \Iresp&\le F(w)
                    -\frac{|\ip{b_H}{f-Hw}|^2}{D_H}.\label{eq:resexact}
\end{align}
If $B=0$, then $b_H=0$, and both corrections are defined as zero,
without division.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof by the positive form]
Put $A=H-aI\succeq0$. Positive-form Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$|\ip{b_H}x|^2\le\kappa\norm{A^{1/2}x}^2$ for
$x\in\Dom(A^{1/2})$. If $\kappa=0$, this inequality makes
$\ip{b_H}x=0$ on the dense form domain, so $b_H=0$.
Otherwise
\[
 H\succeq H_{\min}:=aI+\frac{b_Hb_H^*}{\kappa}.
\]
The variational identity
\[
 \ip f{H^{-1}f}
 =\sup_{x\in\Dom(H^{1/2})}
       \left(2\Re\ip fx-\norm{H^{1/2}x}^2\right)
\]
proves inverse order by comparison with $H_{\min}$; the identity itself
is square completion using the positive square root. Direct multiplication
shows
$H_{\min}^{-1}=a^{-1}I-b_Hb_H^*/[a(a\kappa+\norm{b_H}^2)]$.
This proves \eqref{eq:inverseorder} and \eqref{eq:directexact}.
Apply the same inverse bound to $f-Hw$ in
\eqref{eq:residentity} to obtain \eqref{eq:resexact}.
Finally, $B=0$ implies $b_H=0$ since both summands defining $B$
are nonnegative.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof by bounded inverse slack]
Set $T=a^{-1}I-H^{-1}\succeq0$ and $q_H=Hw$. Then
\[
 Tq_H=b_H/a,\qquad \ip{q_H}{Tq_H}=B/a.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz for the bounded positive form of $T$ gives
\[
 \ip f{Tf}\ge
 \frac{|\ip{q_H}{Tf}|^2}{\ip{q_H}{Tq_H}}
 =\frac{|\ip{b_H}f|^2}{aB}
\]
when $B>0$. This proves \eqref{eq:inverseorder}; application to
$f-Hw$ gives the residual version. Only $Hw$ is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact rescaling and exact equivalence]\label{prop:rescale}
For every $\alpha\in\C$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rescale}
 F((1+\alpha)w)
 =F(w)-\frac2a\Re\!\left(\overline\alpha
                    \ip{b_H}{f-Hw}\right)+\frac{|\alpha|^2B}{a}.
\end{equation}
For $B>0$ the minimizer is
$\alpha=\ip{b_H}{f-Hw}/B$. Its value equals both right sides of
\eqref{eq:directexact} and \eqref{eq:resexact}.
Moreover, the direct bound is sharp among all self-adjoint operators
$\widetilde H\succeq aI$ with this one exact action
$\widetilde H w=Hw$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand \eqref{eq:F} at $(1+\alpha)w$. The quadratic coefficient is
$\norm{Hw}^2/a-\ip w{Hw}=B/a$ and the linear coefficient is the one
displayed in \eqref{eq:rescale}. Completing the complex square proves
the minimizer. To compare the two forms, put $s_f=\ip{b_H}f$.
Then
\[
 \ip{b_H}{f-Hw}=s_f-B,\qquad
 F(w)=\norm f^2/a+B/a-2\Re s_f/a.
\]
Substitution proves equality of the corrected expressions.
For sharpness, $H_{\min}$ from the first proof satisfies
$H_{\min}w=aw+b_H=Hw$ and attains the direct bound.
If $b_H=0$, $aI$ has the required action and attains
$\norm f^2/a=F(w)$. These constructions use no $H^2w$.
\end{proof}

Sharpness here allows every operator satisfying just coercivity and the
one action. It does not establish attainability by the theta realization
or by an operator satisfying all 68 physical action constraints.
Rescaling remains inside the existing span. Under uncertain action data,
the optimizer can depend on the unknown error; no interchange of a
supremum over errors and an infimum over scalings follows. The uniform
bounds below avoid that interchange.

\section{Robust corrections from the saved primitives}\label{sec:robust}

Return to \eqref{eq:maps}--\eqref{eq:scalars}, and write
\[
 k=g-aw=Gc-(a+h)w,\quad b_H=k+z,\quad
 d=a+h,\quad q=a+2h.
\]
The action denominator and overlaps admit formulas involving the same
five primitives. To specify outward bounds, a subscript $-$ or $+$
on a linear expression means the collected interval endpoint described
in Section~\ref{sec:cap}. Define
\begin{align}
 \beta&=u-dx,\qquad \mu_0=u-b-dx+qy,\notag\\
 \mu_-&=(\mu_0)_--hdP_0,\qquad
 \mu_+=(\mu_0)_+,                                      \label{eq:mu}\\
 \rho_U^2&=(m-4u+2qx+4b-4qy+q^2P_0)_+,\notag\\
 N_U^2&=(4b-4qy+q^2P_0)_+,\qquad
 s_{0,U}=(b-qy+hdP_0)_+,\qquad m_U=m_+ .               \label{eq:normlifts}
\end{align}
The notation $\rho_U^2,N_U^2$ denotes rational upper bounds for squared
norms, not the result of squaring previously rounded roots.
Choose outward root bounds
\[
 M_U\ge\sqrt{m_U},\quad
 \mathrm{Rho}_U\ge\sqrt{\rho_U^2},\quad
 N_U\ge\sqrt{N_U^2},
\]
and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DU}
 D_U=a(s_{0,U}+eN_U+e^2).
\end{equation}
In \eqref{eq:mu}, the lower certificate for $p_0$ is exactly zero.
In particular the upper endpoint $\mu_+$ contains no $-hdP_0$.
The signed value $s_{0,U}$ is not clipped.

For $\rho,e\ge0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi}
 \psi(\rho,e)=
 \begin{cases}
  e\rho-e^2,&2e\le\rho,\\
  \rho^2/4,&\rho\le2e.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The definitions agree at equality. Form the real overlap intervals
\begin{align}
 [j_-,j_+]&=[\,\beta_- -eM_U,\ \beta_+ +eM_U\,],\notag\\
 [q_-,q_+]&=[\,\mu_- -e\mathrm{Rho}_U-e^2,\
                    \mu_+ +\psi(\mathrm{Rho}_U,e)\,],     \label{eq:overlaps}\\
 \zeta_d&=\max(0,j_-,-j_+),\qquad
 \zeta_r=\max(0,q_-,-q_+). \notag
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform rank-one bounds]\label{thm:robust}
Under valid primitive enclosures, nonnegative norm upper bounds, and
ordered overlap intervals, one has
\[
 0\le D_H\le D_U,\qquad
 |\ip{b_H}f|\ge\zeta_d,\qquad
 |\ip{b_H}{f-Hw}|\ge\zeta_r.
\]
If $D_U>0$, put $\Delta_d=\zeta_d^2/D_U$ and
$\Delta_r=\zeta_r^2/D_U$. Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BQ}
 B_Q=L_++e^2/a+
 e\min\left(\sqrt[\mathrm{up}]{J_+},
       \sqrt[\mathrm{up}]{W^2}+\frac2a\sqrt[\mathrm{up}]{R_+}\right).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:robustbounds}
 \Iresp\le U:=\min(U_d,U_r),\qquad
 U_d=m_U/a-\Delta_d,\qquad U_r=B_Q-\Delta_r.
\end{equation}
If $D_U=0$, both $\zeta$ values must be zero; set both corrections to
zero and use the uncorrected bounds. A positive $\zeta$ with $D_U=0$,
a negative $D_U$, or a negative final bound is a consistency \hold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Direct expansion gives
\begin{align*}
 \Re\ip kf&=\beta,\\
 \mu:=\Re\ip kr&=\mu_0-hdp_0,\\
 \norm{r-k}^2&=m-4u+2qx+4b-4qy+q^2p_0,\\
 \norm{aw+2k}^2&=4b-4qy+q^2p_0,\\
 s_0:=a\Re\ip wk+\norm k^2&=b-qy+hdp_0.
\end{align*}
Using $0\le p_0\le P_0$ proves the lifted bounds. In particular,
\[
 D_H/a=s_0+\Re\ip{aw+2k}z+\norm z^2
       \le s_{0,U}+eN_U+e^2.
\]
For the direct overlap use
$\Re\ip{b_H}f=\beta+\Re\ip zf$.
For the residual overlap the same error occurs in both factors:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resoverlap}
 \Re\ip{b_H}{f-Hw}
 =\mu+\Re\ip{r-k}z-\norm z^2.
\end{equation}
Its lower bound in \eqref{eq:overlaps} follows from
$-\mathrm{Rho}_U\norm z-\norm z^2
 \ge-e\mathrm{Rho}_U-e^2$.
For the upper bound maximize $\rho t-t^2$ over $0\le t\le e$;
the maximizer is $t=\min(e,\rho/2)$, giving \eqref{eq:psi}.
Thus the two real parts belong to their displayed intervals, and their
distances from zero bound the complex moduli from below.

For any admitted action with $D_H>0$, its exact correction is at least
$\zeta_j^2/D_U$. If $D_H=0$, both true overlaps vanish, forcing
$\zeta_j=0$; the same conclusion holds with the zero-correction
convention. Theorem~\ref{thm:cap} also bounds $F(w)$, not just
$\Iresp$, because its proof already bounds the first line of
\eqref{eq:joint}. Indeed, direct expansion gives
$F(w)=d_0+\norm r^2/a+\norm z^2/a-\Re\ip sz$,
so the same joint and padded estimates bound $F(w)$.
Its outward version is \eqref{eq:BQ}.
Subtracting the uniform lower corrections from the corresponding
uniform upper baselines proves \eqref{eq:robustbounds}. Their extrema
need not occur at the same error vector. If $D_U=0$, nonnegativity of
$D_H$ forces $b_H=0$, proving the degeneracy rule. Finally
$\Iresp\ge0$ explains the negative-bound consistency check.
\end{proof}

The two corrections are never added. Exact-action equivalence in
Proposition~\ref{prop:rescale} does not force the two robust bounds to
agree. A zero certified correction need not mean a zero true
correction. A real-part \emph{upper} bound, in particular, would not
upper-bound the complex modulus.

\subsection{Frozen square-root arithmetic}

The completed rank-one test used $p=128$ fractional binary bits.
Its six root evaluations followed the fixed order
$m_U,\rho_U^2,N_U^2,J_+,W^2,R_+$.
For rational $r=N/D\ge0$, $D>0$, it formed
\[
 k=\left\lfloor\sqrt{\left\lfloor N2^{2p}/D\right\rfloor}\right\rfloor.
\]
The lower root is $k/2^p$; the upper root is the same value if
$k^2D=N2^{2p}$ and $(k+1)/2^p$ otherwise. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rooterror}
 0\le\sqrt[\mathrm{up}]{r}-\sqrt r\le2^{-128},
\end{equation}
and zero returns exact zero. Negative radicands are \hold; they are
not replaced by zero. The frozen test also checks
$W^2=\min(\nu^2t_c,P_0)$ and does not silently replace this minimum
by $P_0$.

\section{An unevaluated precision-only capacity bound}\label{sec:precision}

This section proves a possible follow-up test. It is \emph{not evaluated}
on the saved numbers. Freeze all rational primitive endpoints, $P_0$,
$e$, $a$, and the six radicands of Section~\ref{sec:robust}.
Put $\varepsilon=2^{-128}$. Use hats for the stored outward-root
quantities, and a superscript $\infty$ for the same formulas with exact
square roots of those same frozen radicands. This operation would remove
only root rounding, not primitive Gram radii, trial padding, or physical
action uncertainty.

\begin{lemma}[Root sensitivity of the overlaps]\label{lem:rootsens}
For $j=d,r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zetasens}
 \widehat\zeta_j\le\zeta_j^\infty
       \le\widehat\zeta_j+e\varepsilon,\qquad
 \widehat D-ae\varepsilon\le D^\infty\le\widehat D.
\end{equation}
Also
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BQsens}
 0\le\widehat B_Q-B_Q^\infty
       \le E_Q:=e(1+2/a)\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Replacing $M_U$ by its exact root increases $j_-$ and $-j_+$
by at most $e\varepsilon$. The maximum with zero has the same
monotonicity and Lipschitz bound.
For the residual interval, $\psi(\rho,e)$ is nondecreasing and globally
$e$-Lipschitz in $\rho\ge0$: its derivative is $\rho/2$ below
$2e$ and $e$ above $2e$, with matching values at the join.
Thus reducing $\mathrm{Rho}_U$ by at most $\varepsilon$ increases
$q_-$ and $-q_+$ by amounts in $[0,e\varepsilon]$.
This argument allows a change of branch in \eqref{eq:psi}.
The denominator statement follows directly from \eqref{eq:DU}, since
only $N_U$ changes. Finally the first root branch in \eqref{eq:BQ}
decreases by at most $\varepsilon$ and the second by at most
$(1+2/a)\varepsilon$. The minimum is monotone and changes by no more
than the larger of these two bounds. Multiplication by $e$ proves
\eqref{eq:BQsens}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Capacity of removing only root rounding]\label{thm:precision}
Suppose $\widehat D>0$ and
$D_{\rm floor}:=\widehat D-ae\varepsilon>0$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capacity}
 C_j=\frac{(\widehat\zeta_j+e\varepsilon)^2}{D_{\rm floor}}
                     -\frac{\widehat\zeta_j^2}{\widehat D},
 \qquad j=d,r.
\end{equation}
The possible correction increase satisfies
$0\le\Delta_j^\infty-\widehat\Delta_j\le C_j$.
Consequently, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capacitytest}
 C_d\le\widehat U_d-n,\qquad
 C_r+E_Q\le\widehat U_r-n,
\end{equation}
then even exact radicals cannot make either of these two
\emph{fixed-primitive} bounds strictly less than $n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The numerator bounds and positive denominator floor in
\eqref{eq:zetasens} imply \eqref{eq:capacity}.
The direct baseline $m_U/a$ is unchanged, so
$U_d^\infty\ge\widehat U_d-C_d$.
For the residual bound, \eqref{eq:BQsens} also gives
$U_r^\infty\ge\widehat U_r-(C_r+E_Q)$.
Under \eqref{eq:capacitytest} both exact-root bounds are at least $n$.
They are the most favorable bounds obtainable by replacing only these
outward root approximations with smaller valid ones, by the same
monotonicity.
\end{proof}

If $D_{\rm floor}\le0$, this filter is inconclusive and no division by
that floor is valid. If a capacity upper bound exceeds the relevant gap,
the result is only \open, not \pass. The theorem neither evaluates that
comparison here nor licenses removal of another uncertainty budget.

\section{Several exact actions and one target}\label{sec:higherrank}

The following results are also unevaluated for the saved instance.
They clarify what information additional exact actions could supply.
Let $W:\C^p\to\Dom(H)$ have columns $w_1,\ldots,w_p$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:higherdefs}
 Q=HW,\quad B_{\!W}=(H-aI)W,\quad
 T=a^{-1}I-H^{-1},\quad
 S=aW^*B_{\!W}+B_{\!W}^*B_{\!W},\quad
 \gamma=B_{\!W}^*f .
\end{equation}
Here $S^\dagger$ denotes the Moore--Penrose inverse of the finite
Hermitian matrix $S$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-rank inverse-slack bound]\label{thm:higher}
One has
\[
 S=aQ^*TQ\succeq0,\qquad
 \Ker S\subseteq\Ker B_{\!W},\qquad \gamma\in\Ran S,
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:higherbound}
 T\succeq\frac1a B_{\!W}S^\dagger B_{\!W}^*,\qquad
 \Iresp\le\frac{\norm f^2}{a}
                        -\frac{\gamma^*S^\dagger\gamma}{a}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The identity $TQ=B_{\!W}/a$ gives
$aQ^*TQ=Q^*B_{\!W}=aW^*B_{\!W}+B_{\!W}^*B_{\!W}$.
Let $Y=T^{1/2}Q$. Then $Y^*Y=S/a$ and
$\Ker S=\Ker Y\subseteq\Ker(TQ)=\Ker B_{\!W}$.
The range assertion for $\gamma$ follows by orthogonality to $\Ker S$.
The orthogonal projection onto $\Ran Y$ is
$P_Y=Y(Y^*Y)^\dagger Y^*=aYS^\dagger Y^*$.
Since $0\preceq P_Y\preceq I$,
\[
 T\succeq T^{1/2}P_YT^{1/2}
 =aTQS^\dagger Q^*T
 =\frac1a B_{\!W}S^\dagger B_{\!W}^*.
\]
Taking the quadratic form at $f$ proves the response bound. All
operators involving the square root here are bounded; $Q=HW$ only
requires the original trial domains.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Two-direction Schur gain]\label{cor:schur}
For two columns, suppose $S_{11}>0$ and
$d_S=S_{22}-|S_{21}|^2/S_{11}>0$. Then the correction in
\eqref{eq:higherbound} equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schur}
 \frac{|\gamma_1|^2}{aS_{11}}+
 \frac{|\gamma_2-S_{21}\gamma_1/S_{11}|^2}{ad_S}.
\end{equation}
The deflated action and inverse-slack vectors are
\[
 \widetilde q_2=q_2-\frac{S_{12}}{S_{11}}q_1,\qquad
 \widetilde b_2=b_2-\frac{S_{12}}{S_{11}}b_1,
\]
and
$\ip{\widetilde b_2}f=\gamma_2-S_{21}\gamma_1/S_{11}$.
If $d_S=0$, range compatibility forces this latter overlap to vanish,
and there is no additional gain beyond the first term.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Eliminate the first coordinate of the Hermitian $2\times2$ quadratic
form, or multiply its triangular factorization, to obtain
\eqref{eq:schur}. Because $S_{ij}=a\ip{q_i}{Tq_j}$,
$\ip{q_1}{T\widetilde q_2}=0$ and
$a\ip{\widetilde q_2}{T\widetilde q_2}=d_S$.
Moreover $aT\widetilde q_2=\widetilde b_2$.
The conjugate-linearity of the first inner-product argument accounts
for $S_{21}$ in the scalar overlap, whereas the vector uses $S_{12}$.
When $d_S=0$, positivity gives
$T^{1/2}\widetilde q_2=0$ and hence $\widetilde b_2=0$.
The range compatibility and the one-term correction follow.
\end{proof}

The orthogonality just used is for the $T$ form on the action vectors;
it need not be ordinary Hilbert orthogonality of $b_1,b_2$.
For uncertain data a small or unresolved Schur pivot cannot be used as
a divisor. A valid positive lower certificate is required, or singular
compatibility must be handled explicitly.

\begin{theorem}[A single combined direction for an exact target]
\label{thm:singletarget}
For fixed exact $S,\gamma$ as above, define the quotient to be zero
when $\alpha^*S\alpha=0$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single}
 \max_{\alpha\in\C^p}
 \frac{|\alpha^*\gamma|^2}{a\,\alpha^*S\alpha}
       =\frac{\gamma^*S^\dagger\gamma}{a}.
\end{equation}
If $\gamma\ne0$, a maximizer is $\alpha=S^\dagger\gamma$.
If $\gamma=0$, both sides are zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The range assertion in Theorem~\ref{thm:higher} makes the zero-denominator
convention consistent: the numerator then vanishes.
Write $\alpha^*\gamma=(S^{1/2}\alpha)^*S^{\dagger/2}\gamma$.
Cauchy--Schwarz bounds its squared modulus by
$(\alpha^*S\alpha)(\gamma^*S^\dagger\gamma)$.
For $\gamma\ne0$, the stated $\alpha$ makes this an equality and has a
positive denominator.
\end{proof}

The vector $w_\alpha=W\alpha$ has
$b_\alpha=B_{\!W}\alpha$ and
$a\ip{w_\alpha}{b_\alpha}+\norm{b_\alpha}^2
 =\alpha^*S\alpha$. Thus \eqref{eq:single} says that, for one exact
target, a single combined trial realizes the full finite-rank
correction. It does not provide the needed exact actions or robust
bindings. Applying it to old trial columns with a newly selected
combination would require new coefficient and action contractions and
their validation. No such selection or computation has been made here.

\section{What remains to be measured}\label{sec:ledger}

The saved results establish two limited facts: the original shared-error
cap and its specified fixed-coefficient Young families have an
insufficient common base, while the tested rank-one interval bounds
give positive but insufficient certified corrections. They do not
measure the best possible inverse-slack correction or identify the
largest removable loss.

A future consolidated scalar adequacy ledger could record the following
quantities with separate ownership:
\begin{itemize}
 \item the exact saved baseline gaps, certified lower corrections, and
       remaining margins for each bound;
 \item overlap interval shrinkage, distinguishing a lower certificate
       for improvement from an upper limit on improvement;
 \item the signed denominator components
       $a s_{0,U}$, $aeN_U$, and $ae^2$, retaining the sign of the first;
 \item the precision-only capacities of Theorem~\ref{thm:precision};
 \item a padding-only comparison only after obtaining a valid lower
       certificate for the actual trial norm $p_0$.
\end{itemize}
This ledger is proposed, not evaluated. A lower correction that fails
to close a gap is not a capacity ceiling. Conversely, a proved upper
budget for a specified improvement class that is no larger than its
gap excludes a strict pass by that class; a larger upper budget leaves
the question open. A certified lower gain larger than the gap yields a
pass only when it is safely combined with the corresponding total upper
bound. Independently favorable losses cannot be added without proving
that they are jointly realizable or jointly valid as a bound.

No result here establishes full 372-dimensional positivity, a new
physical theta lower edge, a proof or counterexample to the Riemann
hypothesis, or a general obstruction to other coefficient or
inverse-response methods.

\begin{abstract}
We record a small, conditional improvement of a fixed negative lower bound
for the same minimum theta-weighted self-adjoint operator.
An elementary shift-reuse inequality keeps a previously admitted trial map,
its full-line action errors, all 372 observation coordinates and the
original coefficient matrix. It needs no new source action, coefficient
solve or full trial Gram matrix. We show first that scalar resolvent
continuity using only the old coercivity and response margins gives
exactly the old lower bound. A separately fixed shift beyond that scalar
margin was then tested once with 68 trials. The complete independently
implemented exact checker certified a fresh positive response margin and
an improvement of approximately \(1.29412611030898\cdot10^{-5}\), from
approximately \(-0.0156184544151869\) to \(-0.0156055131540838\).
All analytic-source, operator-domain and physical-inclusion premises remain
explicitly inherited. The new lower edge is negative. Nonnegativity,
a sequence of thresholds tending to zero, and the Riemann hypothesis are
not established. No bibliographical priority is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and the fixed operator}\label{sec:setting}

The object of this note is one fixed lower-bound improvement, not an
unconditional reconstruction of the underlying theta certificate.
The previously archived corrected calculation used 68 trials and all
372 observation coordinates \cite{archive}. We retain those objects and
their coefficient matrix. The new work consists of a shift-reuse argument,
a precise limitation of a scalar-only transfer, and one complete finite
verification at a shift chosen before execution.

For \(x\ge0\), put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta}
 \kappa(x)=e^{x/2}\sum_{n\ge1}(4z_n(x)^2-6z_n(x))e^{-z_n(x)},
 \qquad z_n(x)=\pi n^2e^{2x},
\end{equation}
and extend \(\kappa\) evenly. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hilbert}
 c(x)=\cosh(x/2),\qquad
 \dd\nu(x)=2\kappa(x)c(x)\dd x,\qquad
 \HH=L^2_{\mathrm{even}}(\R,\nu;\C).
\end{equation}
The source normalization is \(\nu(\R)=1\). With
\(\Lambda\) the von Mangoldt function, the symmetric half measure is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:measure}
 \dd\mu(s)=\frac12\frac{e^{-|s|/2}}{1-e^{-2|s|}}\dd s
 +\frac12\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
       \bigl(\delta_{\log n}+\delta_{-\log n}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The value of the continuous density at zero is immaterial; its singularity
is always used with the difference in the form. On the compact smooth
even core, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:form}
 \EE(f)=\iint\kappa(x)\kappa(z)|f(x)-f(z)|^2
                 \dd\mu(x-z)\dd x.
\end{equation}
The notation integrates \(z\) against the translated shift measure,
including its atoms. Equivalently, the positive-shift integral has
continuous weight \(e^{-s/2}/(1-e^{-2s})\) and prime-power weight
\(\Lambda(n)/\sqrt n\), with no extra factor of two.

Let \((\EE,\DD)\) be the \emph{minimum} closure of this core in the
\(\EE+\HH\) norm. Its representing operator is \(A\ge0\). We use the same
operator throughout:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L}
 L=A-\frac12\Id+\frac12P_1,\qquad
 P_1f=\ip1f_{\HH}\,1,\qquad \Dom(L)=\Dom(A).
\end{equation}
Write \(\ell\) for the associated form of \(L\) on \(\DD\).
The inner product is conjugate-linear in its first variable.
No equality of this minimum domain with a maximal jump-energy domain
is assumed.

For orientation, the known source space is the closed span of \(1\) and
\(\kappa^{(2j)}/\kappa-4^{-j}\), \(j\ge1\), in \(\HH\). The present
argument does not assume that this space equals \(\ker L\).
Its lower bound holds on the whole form domain. Restricting it to
\(\DD\cap\NN^\perp\) does not constitute a new use of every source
orthogonality condition to obtain coercivity.

\section{A deterministic response bound and shift reuse}\label{sec:reuse}

We first state a Hilbert-space argument independent of the arithmetic source.
Let \(H\ge a\Id>0\) be self-adjoint, let
\(V:\C^r\to\Dom(H)\), \(O:\C^m\to\HH\), and \(G=HV-E\).
Assume the norm bounds \eqref{eq:errors}. Fix any matrix
\(C:\C^m\to\C^r\) and any \(\tau>0\). No normal equation or optimality
property is imposed on \(C\).

\begin{lemma}[Deterministic response majorant]\label{lem:response}
Put \(D=V^*O\), \(F=\sym(V^*G)\), where
\(\sym X=(X+X^*)/2\), and
\[
 \alpha=\frac{1+\tau}{a},\qquad
 \beta=\nu_0\bar\sigma+\frac{(1+1/\tau)s^2}{a}.
\]
Then, in Loewner order on \(\C^m\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:consumer}
 O^*H^{-1}O\preceq C^*D+D^*C-C^*FC
     +\alpha(O-GC)^*(O-GC)+\beta C^*C.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set \(Y=VC\). Since \(Y\) takes values in \(\Dom(H)\),
expansion gives the exact finite-operator identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
 \begin{split}
 O^*H^{-1}O={}&O^*Y+Y^*O-Y^*HY\\
 &+(O-HY)^*H^{-1}(O-HY).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The inverse is bounded on all of \(\HH\); this identity requires no
action of \(H\) on \(G\) or on \(O\).
Self-adjointness gives
\(V^*HV=F+\sym(V^*E)\). For \(z\in\C^m\),
\[
 |\ip{VCz}{ECz}|\le\nu_0\bar\sigma\norm{Cz}^2.
\]
Writing \(R=O-GC\), deterministic Young's inequality gives
\[
 \norm{(R-EC)z}^2
 \le(1+\tau)\norm{Rz}^2+(1+1/\tau)s^2\norm{Cz}^2.
\]
Use \(H^{-1}\preceq a^{-1}\Id\) in \eqref{eq:identity} and combine
these estimates.
\end{proof}

For the fixed \(H_0,V,G,O\), write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grams}
 M=O^*O,\quad D=V^*O,\quad F=\sym(V^*G),\quad
 K=G^*O,\quad N=G^*G,\quad P=V^*V.
\end{equation}
The archive roles for \(M,D,F,K,N\) are respectively
\texttt{A,D68,E68,K68,N68}. In particular, \texttt{E68} does not denote
the action error \(E\).

\begin{lemma}[Shift reuse with fixed coefficients]\label{lem:shift}
Let \(0<h<\eta\), and keep \(O,V,C\) fixed. Set
\[
 H_h=H_0-h\Id,\qquad \eta_h=\eta-h,\qquad G_h=G-hV,
\]
and define
\begin{align}
 \alpha_h&=\frac{1+\tau}{\eta_h},&
 \beta_h&=\nu_0\bar\sigma+\frac{(1+1/\tau)s^2}{\eta_h},\label{eq:alphabeta}\\
 B_h&=\alpha_h K-(1+\alpha_h h)D,\label{eq:Bh}\\
 \overline S_h&=\alpha_h N-(1+2\alpha_h h)F
       +[\beta_h+(h+\alpha_h h^2)\nu_0^2]I_r,\label{eq:Sh}\\
 \overline U_h(C)&=\alpha_h M-B_h^*C-C^*B_h
                         +C^*\overline S_hC.\label{eq:Uh}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:response-shift}
 O^*H_h^{-1}O\preceq\overline U_h(C).
\end{equation}
The same full-line action error \(E\) is used, and no entrywise enclosure
of \(P\) or second action is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A bounded scalar perturbation leaves both the operator and form domains
unchanged. We have \(H_h\ge\eta_h\Id\) and, on the fixed admitted trials,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:same-error}
 H_hV-G_h=(H_0-h\Id)V-(G-hV)=E.
\end{equation}
The shifted Grams satisfy exact identities
\[
 F_h=F-hP,\qquad K_h=K-hD,\qquad
 N_h=N-2hF+h^2P.
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:response} with \(H_h,G_h,\eta_h\) and expand.
The result is \eqref{eq:Uh} with \(\overline S_h\) replaced by
\[
 S_h=\alpha_h N-(1+2\alpha_h h)F+
             (h+\alpha_h h^2)P+\beta_h I_r.
\]
Since \(P\preceq\nu_0^2 I_r\) and \(h+\alpha_h h^2>0\), this replacement
by \(\overline S_h\) increases the majorant. No positivity of \(F\),
\(S_h\) or \(\overline S_h\) is needed.
\end{proof}

The norm replacement may lose margin. The lemma proves validity, not
sharpness or successful closure. Its proof is an elementary residual
identity and norm estimate; no literature-priority claim is made.
The unchanged \(C\) is a legitimate trial coefficient matrix without
a new solve or a separate optimal-coefficient theorem.

\section{Why scalar continuity alone does not improve the edge}\label{sec:scalar}

Suppose the old full-coordinate certificate gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:old}
 O^*H_0^{-1}O\preceq(1-\mu_0)I_m,\qquad
 0<\mu_0<1,\qquad \delta_*=\mu_0\eta.
\end{equation}
It implies the old lower edge \(b_0=-\delta_0+\delta_*\).

\begin{proposition}[No gain from the two old scalars]\label{prop:nogain}
For \(0\le h<\eta\), spectral calculus gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-transfer}
 I_m-O^*H_h^{-1}O\succeq
          \frac{\delta_*-h}{\eta-h}I_m.
\end{equation}
For \(0\le h\le\delta_*\), the lower bound obtained by combining this
margin with \(H_h\ge(\eta-h)\Id\) is exactly \(L\ge b_0\Id\).
For \(h>\delta_*\), \eqref{eq:scalar-transfer} supplies no positive
response margin.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For \(\lambda\ge\eta\),
\[
 (\lambda-h)^{-1}\le
       \frac{\eta}{\eta-h}\lambda^{-1}.
\]
Functional calculus followed by \eqref{eq:old} proves
\eqref{eq:scalar-transfer}. For the nonnegative margin
\(m_h=(\delta_*-h)/(\eta-h)\), the form argument in
Proposition~\ref{prop:form-transfer} below yields
\[
 L+(\delta_0-h)\Id\ge m_h(\eta-h)\Id
       =(\delta_*-h)\Id.
\]
The \(h\) terms cancel, giving \(b_0\). At \(h=\delta_*\), the
response margin is zero. Beyond it, this particular scalar estimate
cannot certify positivity. This does not assert that the actual response
matrix has lost positivity.
\end{proof}

\section{From finite intervals to the original form domain}\label{sec:finite}

The arithmetic implementation encloses \eqref{eq:Uh} by signed outward
interval operations, retaining inherited physical radii and new rounding
radii. In particular, the negative coefficient of \(F\) reverses endpoints.
Suppose the resulting Hermitian interval matrix has symmetric center \(U_c\)
and symmetric nonnegative radii \(r_{ij}\). Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row}
 U_{\rm cert}=U_c+\diag\Bigl(\sum_jr_{ij}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
For any Hermitian error \(\Delta\) in these radii,
\[
 z^*\Delta z\le\sum_{ij}r_{ij}|z_i||z_j|
 \le\sum_i\Bigl(\sum_jr_{ij}\Bigr)|z_i|^2.
\]
Thus \(U_{\rm cert}\succeq\overline U_h(C)\).
An entrywise upper-endpoint matrix alone would not establish this
Loewner bound.

For \(T=I_m-U_{\rm cert}\), the saved witness consists of a unit-lower
dyadic matrix \(L_f\), a positive diagonal \(D_f\), and an approximate
inverse factor \(M_f\). Suppose exact saved-data checks establish
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witness}
 \begin{gathered}
 \norm{T-L_fD_fL_f^*}_2\le\rho,\qquad
 \norm{L_fM_f-I}_1\le e_1<1,\quad
 \norm{L_fM_f-I}_\infty\le e_\infty<1,\\
 \norm{M_f}_1\le m_1,\quad \norm{M_f}_\infty\le m_\infty,\qquad
 d=\min_i(D_f)_{ii}>0.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The symmetric factor residual can be bounded by its maximum absolute
row sum.

\begin{lemma}[Finite positivity witness]\label{lem:factor}
Under \eqref{eq:witness}, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mu}
 \mu_1=\frac{d(1-e_1)(1-e_\infty)}{m_1m_\infty}-\rho.
\end{equation}
If \(\mu_1>0\), then \(T\succeq\mu_1 I_m\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The Neumann bounds in the \(1\)- and \(\infty\)-norms and
\(L_f^{-1}=M_f(L_fM_f)^{-1}\) give
\[
 \norm{L_f^{-1}}_2^2
 \le\norm{L_f^{-1}}_1\norm{L_f^{-1}}_\infty
 \le\frac{m_1m_\infty}{(1-e_1)(1-e_\infty)}.
\]
Consequently \(L_fD_fL_f^*\succeq(\mu_1+\rho)I_m\).
Subtract the residual bound in \eqref{eq:witness}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Form-domain transfer]\label{prop:form-transfer}
If \(H_h\ge\eta_h\Id>0\) and
\(O^*H_h^{-1}O\preceq(1-\mu)I_m\), where \(0\le\mu\le1\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer}
 L+(\delta_0-h)\Id=H_h-OO^*
      \ge\mu H_h\ge\mu\eta_h\Id
\end{equation}
as forms on the original \(\DD\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(B=H_h^{-1/2}O\). Then
\(\norm{B}^2=\norm{B^*B}\le1-\mu\).
For every \(f\in\DD=\Dom(H_h^{1/2})\),
\[
 \norm{O^*f}^2=\norm{B^*H_h^{1/2}f}^2
       \le(1-\mu)\norm{H_h^{1/2}f}^2.
\]
Subtract this bounded form from that of \(H_h\), and use \(\mu\ge0\).
There is no compression of the conclusion to the trial span.
\end{proof}

\section{The one fixed shifted certificate}\label{sec:result}

The original archived margin satisfies \(0<\mu_0<1/100\), and
\(0<\eta<\delta_0\). Before execution the candidate was fixed as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:candidate}
 h=2\delta_*=2\mu_0\eta,\qquad
 \delta_1=\delta_0-h>0,\qquad \eta_1=\eta-h>0,\qquad \tau=\frac1{100}.
\end{equation}
The 68-by-372 matrix \(C\) was copied byte for byte from the previous
certificate. No coefficient solve, candidate search or change of precision
was allowed.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional fixed-threshold improvement]\label{thm:result}
Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:A}--\ref{hyp:N} and the exact inclusions and
finite witness checked in the public certificate specified in
Appendix~\ref{app:artifact}. Let \(\mu_1\) be its exact rational margin.
Then on the original form domain,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newedge}
 \ell(f,f)\ge b_1\norm{f}^2\quad(f\in\DD),\qquad
 b_1=-\delta_0+2\delta_*+\mu_1(\eta-2\delta_*),
\end{equation}
where \(\ell\) is the associated form of \(L\). Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:improve}
 b_1-b_0=\delta_*+\mu_1(\eta-2\delta_*)>0,\qquad b_1<0.
\end{equation}
The exact \(\mu_1,b_0,b_1,b_1-b_0\) are the fields
\texttt{mu1}, \texttt{previous\_lower\_edge},
\texttt{new\_lower\_edge}, \texttt{strict\_improvement}
of the commit-pinned \texttt{EXACT\_RESULT\_VALUES.json}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The complete interval assembly and Lemma~\ref{lem:shift} give
\(O^*H_h^{-1}O\preceq U_{\rm cert}\).
The checked witness and Lemma~\ref{lem:factor} give
\(I_{372}-U_{\rm cert}\succeq\mu_1 I_{372}\), with
\(0<\mu_1<1\). Proposition~\ref{prop:form-transfer} yields
\eqref{eq:newedge}. Subtracting \(b_0=-\delta_0+\delta_*\)
gives the exact identity in \eqref{eq:improve}; both terms are positive.
The recorded exact numerator of \(b_1\) is negative and its denominator
positive.
\end{proof}

\begin{abstract}
A single finite certification attempt at shift $1/128$ for a minimum
theta-weighted operator stopped at a nonpositive factor pivot. We separate
that failed production from two successful subsequent exact diagnostics.
The first supplies one dyadic direction in which the saved unpadded center
already fails the necessary response inequality. The second bounds the
largest improvement possible by changing the coefficient matrix alone.
For the fixed observation and trial maps, action approximants, norm and
error bounds, trial-Gram majorant, shift and Young parameter, the bound is
strictly smaller than the recorded excess. Thus every coefficient matrix
in this fixed family fails that direction, both for the nominal polynomial
and, conditionally, for the physical upper-majorant family. The nominal
conclusion is also conditional: its positive normal-matrix bound uses an
enclosure bridge from inherited analytic and physical premises.
The argument is elementary square completion with exact saved-data
verification. It gives neither a negative direction of the true operator
nor a general impossibility at shift $1/128$. Source inclusions are not
regenerated, and no conclusion about the Riemann hypothesis is obtained.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and scope}

The earlier fixed-threshold calculation and its subsequent small
fixed-coefficient improvement concern the same minimum theta realization
\cite{archive,shift}. The present result concerns a different, unsuccessful
attempt at $\delta=1/128$. Failure of one coefficient proposal does not
exclude other coefficients. Failure of an upper bound does not determine
the quantity bounded. We therefore distinguish three observations:
the production failure, a saved-matrix direction witness, and a
coefficient-only obstruction for a precisely frozen majorant.

The coefficient obstruction fixes all variables other than the coefficient
matrix. It is not an obstruction after changing the Young parameter,
physical error budgets, observation/trial spaces, action approximants,
trial-Gram upper bound or response inequality. The data/source commit
in Appendix~\ref{app:artifacts} fixes the numerical meaning of these choices.
All new numerical statements in this note are copied from the completed
diagnostics; paper preparation did not repeat their arithmetic.

\section{The operator and inherited premises}

For $x\ge0$ set
\begin{equation}
 \kappa(x)=e^{x/2}\sum_{n\ge1}(4z_n(x)^2-6z_n(x))e^{-z_n(x)},
 \qquad z_n(x)=\pi n^2e^{2x},
\end{equation}
and extend evenly. Put $c(x)=\cosh(x/2)$,
$\dd\nu=2\kappa c\,\dd x$, and
$\HH=L^2_{\mathrm{even}}(\R,\nu;\C)$, with $\nu(\R)=1$.
Inner products are conjugate-linear in the first variable.
The symmetric half measure is
\begin{equation}
 \dd\mu(s)=\frac12\frac{e^{-|s|/2}}{1-e^{-2|s|}}\dd s
 +\frac12\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
       (\delta_{\log n}+\delta_{-\log n}).
\end{equation}
The singular continuous part is always used with the difference in
\begin{equation}
 \EE(f)=\iint\kappa(x)\kappa(z)|f(x)-f(z)|^2\dd\mu(x-z)\dd x.
\end{equation}
Here the $z$ integral includes the translated atoms. The two equal half
contributions give the usual positive-shift formula, with no extra factor
of two. Let $(\EE,\DD)$ be the minimum closure of the compact smooth even
core in the $\EE+\HH$ norm. With its representing operator $A$, define
\begin{equation}
 L=A-\tfrac12I+\tfrac12P_1,\qquad \Dom(L)=\Dom(A),
 \qquad P_1f=\ip1f_{\HH}\,1.
\end{equation}
No minimum/maximal-domain identification is used.

\end{align}

 U(C')=\alpha M-B^*C'-C'^*B+C'^*SC'.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Response majorant and the normal lower bound]\label{lem:response}
Under the inherited premises,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:response}
 O^*H^{-1}O\preceq U(C')\quad\hbox{for every }C',
 \qquad S\succeq m_hI,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mh}
 m_h=\beta-\frac{\sigma^2}{4\tau a}
     =\nu\bar\sigma+76\sigma^2/a>0.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $Y=VC'$ and $T=O-HY$, the operator-domain response identity is
\[
 O^*H^{-1}O=O^*Y+Y^*O-Y^*HY+T^*H^{-1}T.
\]
Write $R=O-G_hC'$, so $T=R-EC'$. Bound $H^{-1}$ by $a^{-1}I$,
apply Young's inequality with parameter $\tau$, use \eqref{eq:budgets},
and bound $-\sym(V^*E)$ by $\nu\bar\sigma I$.
Before replacing $P$, this yields
\[
 C'^*D+D^*C'-C'^*F_gC'+hC'^*PC'
       +\alpha R^*R+\beta C'^*C'.
\]
Expanding $R^*R$ shows that replacing the collected $P$ term by
$P_{\rm up}$ adds $(h+\alpha h^2)C'^*(P_{\rm up}-P)C'\succeq0$,
giving \eqref{eq:majorant}.

For the lower bound, let $u=Vz$, $g=G_hz$. Since $Hu=g+Ez$,
$\norm u\le(\norm g+\sigma\norm z)/a$. Consequently
\begin{align*}
 z^*[\alpha G_h^*G_h-\sym(V^*G_h)+\beta I]z
 &\ge\frac\tau a\left(\norm g-\frac{\sigma\norm z}{2\tau}\right)^2
       +m_h\norm z^2.
\end{align*}
The matrix $S$ is the bracketed matrix plus
$(h+\alpha h^2)(P_{\rm up}-P)\succeq0$.
\end{proof}

\section{The failure direction and three different matrices}

The one production attempt made one new coefficient proposal $C$,
rounded once to exact80-bit dyadics. Its full372 factor stopped at
zero-based pivot210. This did not yet prove that the saved matrix was
indefinite, nor exclude other $C'$.

A subsequent diagnostic constructed one80-bit dyadic direction on
coordinates $0,\ldots,210$, with last coordinate1 and161 exact zero
coordinates appended. Its exact quadratic checker verified
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witness}
 v^*(I-U_{\rm mid})v<0,\qquad v^*(I-U_{\rm cert})v<0.
\end{equation}
Here $U_{\rm mid}$ is the saved100-bit \texttt{U\_MODEL} center, and
$U_{\rm cert}=U_{\rm mid}+P_{\rm row}$ uses all372 columns in every
row-radius sum. Thus removing the final pad alone does not remove this
saved-center failure. This statement concerns stored upper-bound matrices,
not the sign of the true $L$.

For the coefficient argument distinguish:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the exact physical $U(C')$ of \eqref{eq:majorant};
\item the nominal polynomial $Q_0(C')$ obtained from the fixed exact
160-bit dyadic centers $\alpha_0,M_0,B_0,S_0$;
\item the separately rounded stored center $U_{\rm mid}$ and its padded
certificate at the existing $C$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $\alpha_0$ is a scalar-ball center and need not equal exact
$\alpha$. Entrywise requantization means $U_{\rm mid}\ne Q_0(C)$ in
general. The exact check retained
\begin{equation}\label{eq:round}
 d_{\rm round}=v^*U_{\rm mid}v-v^*Q_0(C)v,
 \qquad |d_{\rm round}|\le2^{-100}\norm v_1^2.
\end{equation}
For completeness, the320-to100-bit endpoint quantizer maps a raw center
to either its lower or upper100-bit neighboring point: writing the raw
center and radius modulo $2^{220}$ verifies this directly. Hence each
center error is at most $2^{-100}$, which gives \eqref{eq:round}.
This only controls final center encoding; it removes no physical radius.

Let $c=Cv$, $n=v^*v$ and
\begin{equation}
 r_0=B_0v-S_0c,\qquad e_0=v^*Q_0(C)v-n.
\end{equation}
The saved normal residual stores $S_0C-B_0$, with the opposite sign.
The check independently compared its contraction with $-r_0$ at the
correct scale. Solve inaccuracy and coefficient rounding are already
represented in $r_0$; there is no second coefficient-error allowance.

\section{A bound on every coefficient-only improvement}

\begin{lemma}[Elementary residual bound]\label{lem:residual}
Let $S\succeq mI>0$, $v\ne0$, $b=Bv$ and $c=Cv$. For
\[
 q(y)=\alpha v^*Mv-2\Re(b^*y)+y^*Sy,\qquad r=b-Sc,
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
 q(c)-\inf_yq(y)=r^*S^{-1}r\le\norm r^2/m.
\end{equation}
If $q(c)-n>\norm r^2/m$, every coefficient matrix $C'$ fails
$v^*U(C')v<n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Completing the square gives
\[
 q(y)=\alpha v^*Mv-b^*S^{-1}b
          +(y-S^{-1}b)^*S(y-S^{-1}b).
\]
At $y=c$ the square is $r^*S^{-1}r$, bounded by
$\norm r^2/m$. Every $y$ is attained by
$C'=C+(y-c)v^*/n$, so the coefficient quantifier is unrestricted.
\end{proof}

This is a bound on the greatest possible decrease, not merely an error
estimate for one linear solver. The lemma is standard finite-dimensional
square completion. Its contribution here is its scoped application with
the correct enclosures and exact comparison evidence.

If the physical $S$ lies in $S_0\pm R_S$, put
$\rho_S=\max_i\sum_j(R_S)_{ij}$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bridge}
 S_0\succeq(m_h-\rho_S)I.
\end{equation}
The executed check validated the scalar expression, all68 radius rows and
the raw-input enclosure bridge. The direct center Gershgorin lower bound
$g_0$ was negative. It therefore used a downward160-bit dyadic
$m_0\le m_h-\rho_S$, with the inherited premises explicitly retained.
This is not a premise-free positive-definiteness proof for $S_0$.
An arbitrary matrix in the full interval box has a different obligation,
for example $m_h-2\rho_S$; no whole-box exclusion is claimed.

For the physical comparison, let $R_B,R_M$ be the primitive enclosing
radii and $|\alpha-\alpha_0|\le r_\alpha$. At the same $c,v$, define
\begin{align}\label{eq:errors}
 t&=R_B|v|+R_S|c|,\notag\\
 \epsilon_q
 &=|v|^T\bigl(|\alpha_0|R_M+r_\alpha|M_0|+r_\alpha R_M\bigr)|v|
       +2|c|^TR_B|v|+|c|^TR_S|c|,\notag\\
 R_+&=\sum_i(|(r_0)_i|+t_i)^2.
\end{align}
Then $|q_{\rm actual}(c)-q_0(c)|\le\epsilon_q$ and
$\norm{r_{\rm actual}}^2\le R_+$. With a valid physical lower bound
$S\succeq m_{\rm actual}I>0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:residual} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:physicalgap}
 \inf_{C'}v^*U(C')v-n
 \ge e_0-\epsilon_q-R_+/m_{\rm actual}.
\end{equation}
No \texttt{U\_MODEL} radius or final row pad is added again to
\eqref{eq:errors}. The old coefficient-dependent pad is not fixed across
$C'$. The physical admissible set is not identified with the unrestricted
interval box.

\section{The two conditional exclusions}\label{sec:result}

The accepted exact report records
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tests}
 e_0>\norm{r_0}^2/m_0,\qquad
 e_0-\epsilon_q>R_+/m_{\rm actual}.
\end{equation}
All products use the same $v$. No observed excess is normalized separately
from the improvement bound. The exact lower-bound encodings are
\begin{align}
 m_0&=\frac{11908439363740784705638882337283564911265}{2^{160}},\notag\\
 m_{\rm actual}&=\frac{12019713510740312408761598061526876985806}{2^{160}}.
\end{align}
The first comes from \eqref{eq:bridge}; the second is a downward encoding
of \eqref{eq:mh}. These values are existing report fields, not newly
evaluated approximations.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional obstruction in the frozen family]
Assume the inherited analytic and physical inclusions and use the
commit-pinned data of this note. Keep the same $v$, $\delta=h=1/128$,
$\tau=1/100$, $O_{372},V_{68},G,P_{\rm up}$, norm/action-error bounds,
primitive enclosures and majorant construction. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every unrestricted coefficient matrix $C'$ satisfies
$v^*Q_0(C')v>v^*v$ for the fixed exact nominal polynomial.
\item Every unrestricted coefficient matrix $C'$ satisfies
$v^*U(C')v>v^*v$ for the specified physical-majorant family under its
retained premises.
\end{enumerate}
The nominal conclusion also retains those premises through its lower
bound $m_0$. Neither conclusion determines the sign of $L$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:residual} to the nominal polynomial using the first
strict comparison in \eqref{eq:tests}. Apply \eqref{eq:physicalgap} and
the second strict comparison for the physical statement. The exact
integer form of both comparisons is recorded below and in the public
report. The coefficient quantifier follows from the final step of
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual}.
\end{proof}

Write the saved rational values as
\[
 e_0=E_0/2^{480},\quad\epsilon_q=A_q/2^{480},\quad
 \norm{r_0}^2=Z/2^{640},\quad R_+=P_+/2^{640},
\]
and $m_0=m_0^{\#}/2^{160}$,
$m_{\rm actual}=m_a^{\#}/2^{160}$. The two exact integer margins are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integermargins}
 J_0=E_0m_0^{\#}-Z>0,\qquad
 J_a=(E_0-A_q)m_a^{\#}-P_+>0.
\end{equation}
They give the explicit lower bounds
$J_0/(m_0^{\#}2^{480})$ and $J_a/(m_a^{\#}2^{480})$ for the respective
optimized excesses. Appendix~\ref{app:margins} prints the accepted margin
integers without decimal approximation; the committed JSON supplies
every operand and scale.

The separate sufficient test for existence of a repairing direction,
using an exact row upper bound $L_0$ for $S_0$, was inconclusive.
That failure of a sufficient test does not itself prove an obstruction;
the two independent strict comparisons above do.

\section{Limits and unevaluated follow-up work}

An upper-majorant obstruction does not give a negative direction for
$O^*H^{-1}O$ or for $L$. In particular, it does not exclude another method
at $\delta=1/128$, a changed $\tau$, a different trial/observation map,
sharper source/error information, or a different majorant. No assertion
about all pairs $(C',\tau)$ is made.

A separate reading audit identifies norm, Young, inverse-replacement,
trial-Gram and enclosure allowances. It has not measured their realized
directional sizes or identified a dominant loss. A possible next test is
to keep the current $C,v$ and vary only $\tau$, using scalar contractions
of the saved primitive data. No such numerical test has been run, no
successful parameter has been found, and neither that proposal nor the
loss audit is a theorem or numerical conclusion of this paper.
Directional inverse-response bounds and genuine trial expansion remain
other untested possibilities requiring additional information.

The argument does not reconstruct the source premises, establish
$\NN=\ker L$, prove full-$\NN^\perp$ coercivity, or identify a maximal
operator domain. Internal independent audits are not academic peer review
or formal proof-assistant verification. No literature-priority claim is
made. The status remains \texttt{RH\_PROOF=NOT-OBTAINED}.
